# Independent review of the physical witnesses

**APPROVE** for the selected final single-host normal and race executions. The evidence falsifies the claim that source closure prevents a previously reserved worker's first launch. It also supports the paired-cut, historical-receipt, supported-recovery and ordinary-parent claims for the named programs and endpoint contracts. The observations do not establish arbitrary-schedule recovery or general parent expressiveness.

## Findings and corrections

I rejected the first endpoint instrumentation as behavior-preserving. Reading the body before checking the method/path altered early rejection, and rejecting bodies over 1 MiB altered the original limited decoder's handling of valid padded JSON. I inspected the corrected unjournaled branch against the baseline. It now rejects unsupported methods/paths before reading and uses the original streaming limited decoder. Both corresponding regression leaves passed in the recorded command. The journaled profile deliberately reads and logs a complete bounded body, and the selected drop-after-commit profile deliberately withholds an HTTP response. Neither profile is presumed equivalent on all network inputs. The original source is archived as [rejected endpoint instrumentation](rejected-endpoint-source-v2.go.txt). I also required the results account to distinguish the adapter's retained transport diagnostic from a raw external receipt. Section 04 now makes that distinction.

The final raw result-recorded callback is after the receipt transaction returns and before the separate settlement call. With no observer, Reached returns nil. An enabled observer exposes a real interval and can suspend progress or return an error. Inspection, snapshots and barrier release do not write authority or synthesize a receipt. The adapter's preparation serializes the accepted request without I/O. Its transport performs one POST using the prepared wire and labels the response after checking the correlated receipt. I checked both executor workers' pre-call gate placements, the waiting driver, the resource's journal/Apply/response order, the signal/WaitStatus helper, and the parent program and peer's finite private prefix. The time ordering used below comes from these operations and correlated durable records.

## What the observers distinguish

* **Crash pairs.** Each cut joins Emit history, the original intent, authorization and allowed attempt, request identity/namespace, barrier identity, target and source activation. The two cuts have equal initial and stopped public inspections and every logical table. I separately compared all 43 sqlite_master descriptions and the whole cut replay report within each of the six pairs. No renaming, deletion of timestamps or masking of unequal fields was needed. The model of the observer excludes the signal harness and independent endpoint oracle. U0 has no call at its held worker and no application. U1's matching ingress and committed application precede the response barrier, without a local result, settlement or selection. The operating-system wait status reports a kill for the same PID, barrier token and executable. Recovery starts another PID on the same store, exits successfully and advances the source epoch. The raw idempotent U0/U1 recoveries each add one original-wire POST and select the total-five receipt. U0 makes the first application. U1 returns the original receipt unchanged. The raw non-idempotent and adapter paths remain at the same projection without another ingress or invented observation.
* **Known cuts and replay.** Both routes have a genuine bound result before either known crash. Only the settled cuts already have the selectable observation. Reopened sources consume the exact original observation, joined through consumed sequence and history, and succeed without another contact. Across the 28 replay snapshots per regime, the raw local tables, raw world tables and ingress sequence stay equal. This establishes external silence for these calls. It supplies no independent compiler theorem.
* **Authority and re-exposure.** The 30 authority cases in each regime contain 18 physical POST ingresses, 17 applications, four member effect debits, eight selections of the original effect observation, and 27 accepted lifecycle decisions including the genuine retry. Source and enclosing root are distinct actual subjects. Before first permission the original decision is source_closed or source_retired. First permission survives a closed unscoped source. A denied fresh member reservation has raw reason scope_closed or effect_limit, with no debit or ingress. A held worker on either route can first call after actual source cancellation/retirement. The driver's accepted lifecycle result names the target and changes its status before the worker is released. The released worker subsequently produces the matching ingress. Neither an ingress timestamp nor a pre-call acknowledgment in isolation would order launch. Closed sources cannot select those late results. With just the enclosing root closed, both members do select. Under context-only cancellation, the source remains open with an allowed attempt and empty world. A5 has one earlier committed application, a closed source with no receipt, two byte-identical ingresses, one unchanged application and zero original source selections. The distinct generation Retry has two distinct operation keys, two applications, and a selection in generation two. The non-idempotent and adapter withholding comparisons occur in the crash pairs. The prior-attempt branches in prepareExternalWork and prepareAdapterWork explain withholding independently of closure; the original 30-case matrix contained no corresponding closed-source A5 transport sample on those routes. The separately reviewed supplemental cases below provide those samples.
* **Endpoint and evidence.** The endpoint restart sequence shows five ingresses including a rejected 409, four valid contacts and two durable applications. The first key's duplicate after other-key interference returns its original total five while the ledger total is ten. The second-generation example is a new business attempt. The two private recorder controls retain the original candidate after an identity-conflict refusal for altered key or payload. The successful and deliberately selected-error adapter candidates really contain the endpoint's matching receipt. The actual postcommit connection drop retains status zero and an EOF/transport diagnostic, even though the oracle records application. Unknown mode retains no candidate. Only selected normalized values may drive this ordinary child.
* **Parent and peer.** I joined the original invocation operation to the parent envelope, matching observation ID, consumed sequence and committed history. The success parent selects the forwarded receipt, the two error parents select failed/member_failed without a response, and the unknown parent remains open at PC 2 with no selected child outcome. Each noncrash peer case orders primary delta one before peer delta two, with the peer's own successful result selected at capacity one. In the private-failure schedule a completed parent has already selected failed/member_failed at a coherent cut with an allowed child attempt and no result. The worker is held before its delegate can POST. Its eventual receipt is retained without reopening either control. In the separate crash parent pair the unknown child has no application and neither control fabricates a result. The recorded child completes through its genuine result and parent selection without another world contact. The crash cases have no peer.
