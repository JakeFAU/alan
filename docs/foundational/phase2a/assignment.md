2. Phase 2 charter: the model meets an independent world
The overarching question is:
Can the public runtime's retained computation, evidence, and authority support useful and justified action when external consequences can outlive the process and may not be reflected in its surviving local record?

Pillar IV supplies the meaning of the split-phase effect protocol and its evidence. Pillar V asks what continuation remains justified after volatile state disappears. Phase 2 should ultimately reconnect both to a meaningful effectful composition. A successful HTTP call and a separate replay demonstration do not close that combined question.

Judge the program on four obligations:
1. Adequate distinctions: local error, external rejection, completed effect, absent observation, and unresolved outcome have the meanings the evidence supports.
2. Sound claims and actions: no invented result, unsupported claim of nonexecution, unauthorized continuation, or repetition forbidden by the declared effect contract.
3. Conditional progress: when the stated service and endpoint capabilities make continuation possible, execution actually uses them.
4. Useful composition: parents can carry the relevant obligations and outcomes through ordinary interfaces; identify where they currently cannot.
Uncertainty may be unavoidable. A system that always refuses, always fails, or always remains unknown has not thereby established useful recovery. Conversely, a missing result does not authorize a retry. Explicitly identify which additional endpoint capability, authority, or observation changes what is possible.

Treat ownership of a computation separately from transactional control over its effects. A parent's failure does not erase an already applied child effect. A compensation is another effect with its own possible uncertainty. A recorded historical receipt does not automatically describe a mutable resource's current state.

For a nondeterministic result, replay of retained bytes and generating a new result are different events. A result's provenance does not prove every assertion in its contents. No live model call is required for this first campaign.

5.1 Events and counts

Event	Evidence and question
Intent commitment	Original source, sequence, payload and operation are retained; no remote action follows from commitment alone.

Attempt reservation	Exact request/preparation, retained decision and any applicable effect charge.
Worker entry	A worker begins handling reserved work.
Launch	Define precisely for each route; distinguish HTTP transport invocation, actual request transmission, and adapter Execute entry.
Endpoint arrival/application	Independent request arrival and durable remote mutation are different events.
Evidence acquisition/recording	A volatile response differs from a durably retained correlated result.
Settlement and consumption	A result can be retained or admitted without a live continuation selecting it.
Logical closure	A genuine authorized lifecycle transition targeting a specified source or scope.
Process crash/reopen	Verified process loss and a fresh activation under the actual epoch protocol.


Keep reservations, logical intents, physical launches, endpoint contacts, applications, receipts, and resource charges as separate counts. Do not assume one equals another. State which count each budget actually limits.

Also distinguish a candidate with disposition unknown, which the current adapter path does not retain as a result. Do not silently equate these cases or infer remote nonexecution from a field named “known,” absence from a missing-result list, or a selectable failure envelope.

5.3 Cancellation and recovery
Distinguish actual source cancellation, enclosing-root closure with the source still open, retirement where materially different, runner-context cancellation, and process death. Verify the before/after state and exact operation. An accepted already_terminal no-op is not a new closure.

Separate permission for further actions from facts about earlier actions. A local cancellation may prevent some future steps while an in-flight request still applies remotely. An earlier launch finishing later does not refute a claim forbidding a new launch after cancellation. A retained allowance is not proof of a current physical fence.

Reopening the same logical operation is different from a new execution generation or a new business request. Compare those identities explicitly. Deduplication of one key does not imply deduplication of the same business action under another key. Do not promise exact expenditure or identical future schedules merely because a recovered public value agrees.
6. The independent world and observer boundary

9. Review focus and stop conditions
Reviewers must challenge:
- A reference that merely restates implementation outcomes or omits eligible work.
- Oracle ground truth leaking into the runtime or parent.
- A “crash” that preserves the supposedly lost volatile result.
- Permission/reservation mistaken for physical launch; arrival mistaken for application.
- Cancellation mistaken for remote nonexecution, or a saved allowance mistaken for a current fence.
- A recorded error or typed default mistaken for knowledge of the external mutation.
- Endpoint deduplication, query, authentication, persistence or fencing stronger than the tested contract.
- Host inspection or a custom wrapper credited as an ordinary agent capability.
- New-generation business repetition mistaken for recovery of the original operation.
- Safety achieved by permanent refusal being promoted to useful recovery.
