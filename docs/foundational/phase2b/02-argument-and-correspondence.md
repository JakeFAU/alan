# Finite dispatch-authority model and correspondence

## Domain fixed before enumeration

The prospective bounds are in [the frozen matrix](01-source-and-matrix.md). The exact profile manifest is `research/dispatchauthority/profiles.go`. A state starts just after permission, one reservation, and any member debit, with one prepared opportunity for generation 1/revision 1 in namespace N. The duplicate profile starts after *both* permitted plain-store keyed direct callers prepared their copy, before either gate. There is one operation key, immutable request/preparation bytes, one historical permission in epoch 1 and one debit for a member (zero for an unscoped subject). These common prefixes are assumed. No profile tests initial permission, budget exhaustion or the compiler. The controlled adapter has one keyed POST per sending Execute. The raw endpoint is keyed except in `raw-plain`. The foreign world keeps at most two independently serviced packets bound to the same immutable request, with at most two owner activations, two opportunities and one optional peer private commit. There are no unbounded retry, fairness or distributed takeover claims.

The 21 profiles separate the following choices. A run may take any enabled local or external step, or stop at any finite prefix. The one ordinary peer is independent and needs no physical effect slot.

|Names|Environment and monitored authority|

## Transition table and exchange format

The gzipped JSONL export contains one graph `{Profile,Rows}` per line. Each row has a consecutive `ID`, a full `State`, and labelled `Outgoing` edges `{Action,To}`. ID 0 is initial. Local fields contain the binding, one reservation, durable projections and evidence. `Owner`, `Epoch`, the two `Worker` slots and `Sticky` describe the local owner. `Ack` is a history monitor (one successful Q per trace). `Wire[2]` and `Applications` describe the independent world. Numeric worker suffixes `.0`/`.1` identify opportunities, never semantic keys. No export row supplies its own expected assertion or a runtime fixture. Candidate policies and falsifiers are evaluated independently from observed traces.

* `operator.<closure>` applies while owner-open and source/scope is still in its initial state. Cancel, abandon and retire set the original source to cancelled, abandoned or cancelled+retired. Natural failure/End set failed/succeeded. Nested member failure simultaneously installs its non-success freeze. Sole root End immediately closes the root scope. `member-end` in the alias comparator leaves the scope open. Both End profiles label the action `operator.end`. Retry is enabled only after the original has failed and advances the current generation to 2 while keeping the original terminal projection. Top-root closure and nested failure close only the modeled scope. Nested retirement/publication first take `operator.freeze-success` to a successfully frozen but unpublished scope. Retirement may close it immediately. Successful publication also requires selected captured descendant evidence (the remaining ordinary return, descendant successful End and publication steps are compressed, so the original source is marked succeeded). None of these changes edits the historical grant or debit.
* `gate.admit.i` changes a ready descriptor to admitted in one step on an open owner iff its source and applicable scope are eligible. `gate.refuse-<reason>.i` consumes the ready descriptor on refusal. Precedence is generation, retirement, source terminal, scope, with owner closing taking precedence over logical checks. Revision is a fixed provisioned constant in this domain; corruption is outside it. Only `open` and `success-unpublished` scopes are eligible. A checked-but-unregistered token is unrepresentable. Source Q checks the same logical predicate, scope Q only effective scope closure.
* An admitted `worker.enter.i` reaches L. Alternatively `worker.withdraw.i` queues an empty result before L on owner closing. `worker.send.i` from entered/no-packet transmits. `endpoint.ingress.i` advances sent to accepted and `endpoint.serve.i` independently applies or deduplicates an accepted packet. The keyed endpoint increments Applications only on its first service for this operation. Neither endpoint action reads owner state. `worker.return.i` on served queues authentic evidence. The loss profile instead permits `worker.loss.i` anywhere after L, regardless of transmission or application, without deleting a packet. Neither Execute nor Do implies ingress. The model omits transport-internal steps other than send and the declared cuts.
* `account.record.i` moves known bytes to recorded and ensures the durable original record exists. `account.settle.i` moves recorded to finished H and settles the bound record, including a duplicate that was already settled. In the fault profile `account.error.i` finishes H with a sticky accounting error, leaving the real bytes durably recorded. `account.unknown.i` finishes H for an empty queue entry and supplies no receipt. Every admitted, entered, queued or recorded worker remains individually live until H. A normal Run or the observer can service `recovery.settle` when genuine recorded evidence survives without a live handler. `source.select` consumes only settled evidence on the open original; non-awaiting natural End/Failure fixtures have no such transition. `peer.commit` is independently enabled on an open owner in peer profiles.
* `owner.begin-close` prevents A. The original owner drains all prepared/live workers, including a direct call held before A, before `owner.release`. `owner.kill` drops both worker slots and the volatile failure map but preserves the durable tuple, packet and application history. One `owner.open` from released/dead starts epoch 2. A keyed raw missing result supplies a fresh ready descriptor in slot 1 requiring a new gate. An old plain raw or adapter reservation with a missing result yields no such descriptor. Known evidence supplies settlement/selection with zero new transport. Reopening alone supplies no world evidence.
* `observe.Q` marks success only on an open owner, after exact target revocation, with no live worker, sticky error or unsettled record. Prepared descriptors can still exist and be refused after Q. A matching sticky error, temporary idleness of a still-eligible target, pending work and timeout have no affirmative Q edge. The state-derived `Wait` observations in the exporter/checker distinguish those refusals. There is no active-Run prerequisite. `Ack` never arms a gate or drives evidence handling.

## Safety and information arguments

Fix one immutable original run and, for a scope wait, a genuine alias naming the affected scope. In the nested profiles the acting source is a descendant in a distinct invocation. A member of the *enclosing* frame provides the scope alias, and the descendant belongs to its immutable caller chain. The `Scope` field projects effective closure on this chosen chain. An unscoped source's placeholder scope stays open. All finish/selection and application claims below concern this one operation. A successor would require a different operation and is outside this state space.

1. **R excludes later A.** In the projection the only edge that creates an admitted worker atomically replaces one ready descriptor after checking its own source and effective scope. Closing facts are monotone for the original run: an original non-open projection never becomes open again, retirement persists, Retry changes the current generation, and an enclosing non-success freeze, closure or publication persists. An unpublished successful nested freeze is eligible. Thus after effective R every fresh gate for this run must refuse. This proof uses the actual durable R of the targeted authority. Temporary owner shutdown while a logical source remains eligible cannot serve as R.
2. **Q excludes later L.** L requires an admitted slot. Every admission before R adds its own live slot before another transition can observe it. R prevents further successful admission and Q checks all matching slots. H removes one slot, and no edge derives an admitted slot from a finished worker or a saved permission. Death discards every old volatile slot. A future owner must acquire the flock, obtain a new descriptor if the route permits, and check eligibility again. These premises give the ordering by induction, including two simultaneous copies of one key. If A wins before R, L is still enabled. `gate.admit.0, operator.cancel, peer.commit, worker.enter.0` is a counterexample to a post-R physical fence.
3. **No loss of available evidence at Q.** An acquired authentic response is represented by a live queued or recorded worker until handling finishes. A successful finish records the immutable bound evidence and settles it. The alternative settlement-error H leaves both durable evidence and a matching owner-local error. Q cannot succeed in that state. If the owner died after recording but before settlement, the new empty registry is insufficient. `recovery.settle` must process the existing durable evidence first. A handler with no available response can finish with an unresolved original obligation. A source that already terminated need not select any observation. The argument assumes trusted correlation, successful durable commits when reported, and the previously recorded receipt survives owner loss.
4. **Binding and resource conservation.** All transitions preserve the initial durable key, request, grant, historical epoch/revision and reservation/debit. Reopen changes `Epoch` for the new owner, never the request. This is a syntactic invariant of the model's transition table. The integer `Reservations` counts the original semantic reservation. A new physical opportunity and any separate scheduler expenditure do not increment it. The direct profile has zero member debit and still has exactly one durable operation reservation.
5. **The two worlds at refusal/Q.** After the same admitted prefix, cancel the original under both histories. Kill U0 before L. In U1 perform L, send, ingress, application and validated response acquisition, then kill before recording. These additional actions write no local durable row; death clears the two volatile slots on both sides. The complete abstract local states coincide and the application counts are respectively zero and one. After reopen both new attempts at the old keyed route are refused, Q can succeed on both, and neither acquires a record. A claim of prior nonapplication or application based solely on the shared local facts must be wrong in at least one of the two worlds. This inference requires the specified no-reconciliation environment. It says nothing about evidence a different endpoint could legitimately provide. A second schedule takes R after ingress, then kills, opens, acknowledges Q, and serves the accepted packet. Here even *future* remote nonapplication after Q is false.

## Conditional service and concrete correspondence

Assume responsive local mutex and transaction service, a live scheduler that chooses eligible ordinary work, a bounded or cooperatively withdrawing transport, sufficient local budget and eventual successful accounting. R and the one peer commit need no endpoint return in the short-gate profiles. In the real automatic runner a synchronous pre-A pause occupies its ordinary turn; realizing the competing R there needs another genuine public driver. With the worker held *after* A, the same automatic runner is free to commit ordinary work. A single occupied effect slot can still delay another external peer. The model peer is purely local. It makes no statement about an external peer or general fairness. `TestShortcutControls` holds a real mutex across synthetic transport and challenges it with independent revocation and peer acquisition attempts. Both remain blocked until the call ends. Its separate last-read and split-registration controls use ordered channel handshakes. They are small executable counterexamples to the proposed shortcuts, separate from observations of the production Store.

Q requires service for every remaining covered worker, its queue and any retained original record. With a matching accounting failure it returns the error. A deadline can expire before any of these steps and conveys no certificate. For open supported raw keyed recovery, every maximal fully serviced extension from either U0/U1 selects a genuine receipt, with one total application. In the other open profiles no second physical action is enabled by the existing transport policy. The original remains unresolved on both worlds. In the closed keyed pair every serviced extension refuses the fresh physical opportunity, preserves the previous application count and can acknowledge Q. `TestRecoveryCutsAndAllServicedMaxima` checks the full reachable suffix from each of those eight reopen states, including the all-eligible-actions maximality criterion. Requiring observation service forces the optional host acknowledgment on the maximal closed suffix; abstaining from calling Q leaves the same semantic refusal. A profile sink counts *all* labels, including optional operator actions, optional Q, owner crash/open and independently serviceable endpoint packets. A live Store can finish an ordinary turn even if one of those external choices remains. Endpoint service is an additional hypothesis when claiming completion; a held packet may remain unapplied for an arbitrary prefix.

Opening a new eligible owner can expose the same retained raw keyed request with a new gate, subject to the existing validated store and exclusive single-host OS flock. A selected endpoint may keep a packet after it has acknowledged ingress; it can proceed independently of killing or reopening the local Store.|
