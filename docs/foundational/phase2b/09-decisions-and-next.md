# Decision and next research task

**Pillar IV.** The single-host dispatcher can revoke current admission without holding a lock across untrusted-duration I/O. A worker that won admission before revocation can still first enter its call later. Distinct lifetimes through H allow a separate local drain to account for that cost. A current-open-source gate makes terminal private Failure and ordinary successful End consequential. After a source succeeds, its aggregate can remain pending on a captured unresolved effect. A successful unpublished nested frame still permits eligible descendants, and a late correlated result can outlive the authority to issue a new call.

**Pillar V.** Recovery can truthfully settle surviving evidence, and eligible same-key raw resubmission can support useful progress under an exact-request idempotent contract. Revocation can prevent that resubmission without revealing the missing remote fact. Two complete retained histories at the revoked cut can agree and yield Q while their applications differ. A packet accepted before death can apply after fresh-owner Q. General recovery simulation, deliberately interfering composition and distributed ownership remain open.

**Next substantial task.** Specify and falsify an original-operation reconciliation capability. Fix who can request evidence after the original effect authority ends, how an independent endpoint binds the answer to the original key and bytes, and what a still-in-flight answer means. A decisive answer must advance the original obligation without silently issuing a new business mutation. Test both matched revoked histories and the held packet that applies later. An early `not found` treated as final nonexecution, any invented receipt, or any new physical mutation under a read-only entitlement would falsify the proposed contract. Include a positive case in which an ordinary continuation can actually use legitimately retained evidence. Choosing which public actor receives that capability and which outcomes an ordinary parent may rely on belongs to the next specification gate.
