# Historical lifecycle decisions

## Contract and qualification boundary

The target of a lifecycle request is a **subject name**. A successful lookup returns the subject’s then-current logical generation and its run. A stale requested generation, a closed run, a retired subject, or a denied target grant can all have a target witness. Only a missing subject gives the owner a `target_missing` decision with no before or after witness.

1. A reduction commits an immutable lifecycle intent naming its original source, generation, target, requested generation, operation and expected revision.
2. The source authorizes that intent at a retained epoch, sequence and revision. A source refusal cannot produce a target decision. An authorized request may still be waiting for its target decision.
3. The owner looks up the target under its single-host lock and SQLite transaction. It evaluates the existing target policy and retains the original request, decision sequence, reason, authority witnesses and any consequential change atomically. Scoped facts cover the source root and the root of a target that actually participated. Settlement and observation selection have their own evidence.
4. Static subjects exist from provisioning. Invocation members first exist at their original admitted creation. A retry changes the current generation of an existing subject. Closure, retirement and process death preserve the original logical identity and its retained history.
5. Inspection and Replay read a single committed cut. Replay independently checks the program reductions, actual observation selections, emitted identities, debits, original decisions and protocol evidence. Owner reopen validates that same persisted history before renewing each still-open generation exactly once in its existing transaction. A rejected reopen grants no activation and changes no logical records, balances or durable WAL frames.

An authenticated target witness establishes presence at the lookup. A genuine recorded missing lookup establishes absence at that boundary under the runtime writer contract. If the named member exists in the cut, its later creation must be compatible with **all** retained constraints: original scoped decisions, each journal's own append order, cross-journal identities, atomic record groups, membership, closure, and the epochs shared by overlapping open runs. Two generations can end at the same epoch after different lifetimes. A positive creation or transport witness establishing that the target already existed falsifies the absence.

A missing decision record leaves the lookup unresolved. The validator cannot supply a missing witness or discard one to make a history pass. When independent records establish no order, their relative order remains unknown. Validating a recorded lookup against the constraints does not independently date it or establish a unique schedule. Observationally indistinguishable, coherently replaced evidence requires external provenance. Physical timestamps confer no authority.

## Frozen controls

* **Genuine absence.** An authorized controller receives a missing decision before a legal invocation creates the named member. Keep the original operation, request and child identities, source and target decisions, charges, selection evidence and local status. All three public readers/owners must accept this cut subject to their resource limits and the owner lock.
* **Forged absence.** Execute a second genuine schedule in which that same member already exists through the lookup. Preserve the genuine source store. In a disposable copy, substitute a canonical missing result and remove the target-only decision fact, or rebind its scope where applicable. Match the positive cut's final epoch counters. All three operations must reject if earlier durable evidence proves presence. A rule that trusts a null witness, assumes every current member always existed, or relies on equal final epochs fails these controls.
* **Authority and crash.** Check scoped and unscoped requests, original subjects, missing names that never appear, retired/closed/superseded generations, a logically open worker after SIGKILL, and repeated reopens. Acknowledged transaction boundaries separate pending authorization/lookup, durable decision, admission, and activation. Inspection and Replay cannot spend, deliver, settle or contact an endpoint. Failed reopening must roll back even a partially staged renewal.
* **Storage.** Ordinary formats 3 and 6 contain no invocation member-creation protocol. Formats 4 and 7 include finite composition. Formats 5 and 8 additionally include receipt reads. Historical records are interpreted under the protocols actually present. The repair must preserve the existing schema, external keys and public signatures.
* **Completion gate.** Preserve the paired witnesses, reject deliberate authority, identity and order mutations, compare independent finite-history checks, and qualify all three APIs from the same immutable source. Stop delivery for unexplained disagreements, durable side effects, a lost original obligation, or a substantiated unresolved review finding.

Milestone 2's merged source (tree `9668a9f089b38bdb378e8b7eed69eea2e0f66295`) accepted the genuine later-created member in typed inspection. Both deterministic Replay and owner reopen rejected it with `lifecycle target existence`. This is the baseline that the frozen controls distinguish.

## Worked example

An application authorizes a controller to request cancellation. The controller selects input `control-target` and emits `["retirement-controller",1,2,0]`. Its source authorization succeeds. At target lookup the named entry has no subject yet. The owner retains `target_missing` and no target witness. The root then admits its original invocation, creates that entry at generation 1, and the entry consumes its invocation request. The earlier refusal still awaits its own settlement.

After SIGKILL, inspection and deterministic Replay can read both events. A fresh owner validates the history and advances the activations of open generations. The worker retains its original identity and selection. A separate non-idempotent write keeps its original reservation and unknown result. A process exit supplies no evidence that the worker logically terminated or that the remote write took effect.

The paired execution starts from the same provisioned bytes. It creates the entry first. A transport binds to that generation **before** the controller input enters the admissions journal. The entry's original request also appears before that input in the observations journal. The lookup retains `grant_denied` with an open target witness. A disposable copy replaces this result with `target_missing`, removes the target-only fact and restores contiguous fact numbering. All three APIs reject while either journal still proves earlier presence, even though the genuine schedules end with identical per-run epochs. The [witness manifest](../runtime/testdata/lifecycle-recovery/README.md) identifies the original stores, each edited copy and the order-reversal controls.

## Evidence and limits

The validators relate original operation, subject, generation and observation identities. They use retained append order **within** a journal, records committed by the same operation, and activation counts for runs open over overlapping intervals. SQLite row numbers in different tables do not share a clock. A closed generation stops acquiring activations. Creating a member or retry successor starts its own lifetime.

These checks assume the ALAN writer and an intact retained cut. If an attacker coherently replaces every witness distinguishing two schedules, the database can have the same observable facts as a genuine execution. The independently enumerated [bounded examples](../internal/executor/testdata/recovery-model.tsv) include that pair. The public APIs supply neither an external attestation of a database's origin nor a total ordering for unrelated transactions.

Inspect and Replay take read-only snapshots. Open first checks the retained journal mode on a reader which stays alive across failed validation, including after loss of the last owner. A separate, short-lived connection stages the epoch updates under the same host lock. Its dirty pages stay in memory until commit so rejection during validation or staging preserves the database and original WAL bytes. Memory needed for renewal grows with the pages it touches. SQLite may rebuild its shared-memory index. A physical I/O failure during commit can leave an uncommitted WAL suffix. In file-size fault tests, the original committed prefix and epochs survived. Successful recovery renews the open generations exactly once and gives the caller the usual execution connection. No adapter registration is necessary for any of these checks.
